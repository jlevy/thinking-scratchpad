# `s(20)` — open

Open. The best known packing gives `s(20) ≤ 5`. The verified lower bound is
`s(20) ≥ 24/5 = 4.8`, from this repository’s weighted fractional unavoidable-set
certificate at that side (`T-020`, 2026-09-04, `V4/C4`), leaving a gap of `0.2`. It
displaced Nagamochi’s general `1 + √13 ≈ 4.605551`, which had held this case since 2005,
by `0.194449`. This is the first lower bound of any kind proved about 20 squares in
particular rather than read off a formula.

## The packing

The record catalogue does not picture `n = 20`: no arrangement has ever been found that
beats the trivial `⌈√20⌉ = 5` grid, so the grid is still the best known packing.
That is a statement about what has been searched, not a proof.

## The lower bound

The operative bound is a weighted fractional unavoidable-set certificate at side `24/5`:
2260 rationally weighted atoms on a `D4`-symmetric site set, total mass
`946131/50000 < 20`, every closed `B`-square at every net direction capturing mass at
least one, the least being `50007/50000`. It is decided from its own bytes by an exact
event-cell sweep and by an interval branch and bound over centre boxes, which agree on
that least value to the digit.
The certificate was generated for `n = 20`, and it carries this case with no
monotonicity step: of the five conditions only `Condition 2` mentions `n`, so an atom
set certifies its side for every integer strictly above its own mass, which is 19 and
upward. Nagamochi’s general closed form was the whole of what was known here until
2026-09-04 and remains the external published baseline, applying to every `N ≥ 4`:

```
s(N) ≥ min{ ⌈√N⌉,  √(N − 2⌊√N⌋ + 1) + 1 }
```

It is still the default across almost the whole open frontier — 58 of the 65 open cases
at `n ≤ 100` are governed by it, down from 60 before this result — and outside those
displacements it has not been improved since 2005.

## What the method can still add here

A certificate for `n` cannot exist above `⌈√n⌉·B`, which at `B = 9977/10000` is
`4.9885`, so `0.1885` of structural runway remains above `24/5`. Unlike `n = 19`, no
packing binds first: the best known packing here is the trivial grid at `5`. So this
method could in principle close the case to within `0.0115` of the upper bound, and can
never reach it.
